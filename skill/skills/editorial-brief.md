# Skill: editorial brief (before any title or post)

One page that every post follows. Run it right after `start` (or after a research folder is checked), before `plan` and before writing.

1. `CW brief <run>` writes `editorial-brief.md` in the run folder with five sections:
   - reader: one kind of person, as specific as you can (what they do, what they know, what they want).
   - purpose: one sentence: after reading, the reader knows / can do / feels ...
   - claims to keep: the points every post must keep, one per line, each from the source. Numbers exactly as the source says them, and the same direction (늘었다 stays 늘었다).
   - what may be public: the material you may show (their video frames, their page, numbers they said). Anything private stays out: other people's names, client work, prices they did not say.
   - next action: the one thing the reader does next (watch the video, sign up, reply). One line.
2. Fill it from the source and what the user told you. Ask the user only when the reader or the next action is not clear from what they said.
3. `CW brief <run> --check` fails an empty section, more than one next action, a number that is not in the source, a number that goes the other way from the source, or a quote that is not the source's words. Fix the brief, never the rule.
4. Then `CW plan <run>`: it fills "for who", "angle" and "next action" from the brief. Every title and first line serves that reader. `write` (and you, writing post.md) follows the brief: keep every claim, show only public material, end with the next action.

The idea of a creative brief before writing is ordinary editorial practice. The five sections and the checks are ours.
